# Demand-side critique — five refined candidates

Ranking by the strength and breadth of the real human need served. Shippability and
technical elegance deliberately ignored; the question is what a customer wants and what
would make a judge feel the entry matters.

## RANKING

1. **care-circle** — The deepest and most recurrent pain on the list: a caregiver who cannot
   answer "what is my parent taking?" — health, guilt and money at stake, in a huge and
   growing population, with a one-page brief a judge can picture handing to a doctor.
2. **household-paper-trail** — The broadest need — every household's pile of policies, bills,
   receipts and warranties — with real money at stake (lapsed cover, denied claims, missed
   renewals) and the clearest artefact: what expires in 90 days, what is unverified, what is
   missing.
3. **incident-evidence-vault** — The highest-stakes episodic need — a break-in, a stolen
   parcel, a damage dispute — and the only concept that makes cameras matter, but it fires
   rarely and serves a narrower owner.
4. **photo-time-repair** — A universal, chronic annoyance (a library stuck at 1970-01-01) with
   a lovely before/after, but it is a nuisance rather than a crisis, and date-repair tools
   already exist.
5. **the-11pm-file-rescue** — Universal but trivial: finding a file is friction, not a pain,
   and the demo is a search box — the least cinematic, least meaningful result on the list.

## THE STAKES

At 2 a.m. in A&E, the doctor asks what your father takes — and instead of a shoebox of
packets and a panicked guess, you hand over a one-page brief.

## WHO ELSE LOSES

- **incident-evidence-vault** — Without it, a judge sees no answer to "what are my cameras
  actually for?": no local, private way to turn a break-in or a stolen parcel into evidence
  an insurer or a police officer will accept, and the NAS's most dramatic capability stays a
  passive recording.
- **household-paper-trail** — Without it, the most universal need on the list stays unmet:
  every household's policies, warranties and bills remain a pile, and the one artefact a
  judge could read in five seconds — what expires in 90 days, what is unverified, what is
  missing — never exists.
